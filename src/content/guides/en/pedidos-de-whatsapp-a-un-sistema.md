## Why the chat gets hard at rush hour

WhatsApp works well while orders are few. When many arrive at once, almost every diner or restaurant runs into the same problems:

- The same questions over and over: is a dish available, how much is it, do you deliver.
- Orders spread across several messages, sometimes with changes halfway through.
- Nobody knows for sure which order is cooking and which has already left.
- The customer asks "is it on its way?" and someone has to search the chat to answer.

An ordering system does not replace service: it organizes the repetitive part so your team can handle the rest better.

## What changes in each part of the business

| Area | With the chat | With a system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer | Asks for the menu and prices | Sees the current menu and builds the order |
| Kitchen | Gets the order by word of mouth or on paper | Sees every order with its time and status |
| Register | Adds it up by hand | Sees each order’s total |
| Follow-up | Replies message by message | The status says whether it is cooking or ready |

## One step at a time

1. **Organize the menu.** Dishes, prices and what sells out early. It is the base for everything else and helps even if you only use WhatsApp for now.
2. **Define the statuses.** For example: new, cooking and ready. If your team already uses them out loud, the system just makes them visible.
3. **Try it with a small group.** Share the link with trusted customers before announcing it to everyone.
4. **Keep WhatsApp for notices.** You do not have to close the channel your customers already use; the change is that orders are taken in one place.
5. **Review after a week.** Look at which questions still reach the chat and adjust the menu or the instructions.

## Before choosing a system

- Can your customers order from their phone without downloading an app?
- Does the kitchen see orders on a screen or on the phone it already has?
- Can you change prices and dishes without asking anyone for help?
- Do they show you a demo with your own menu before you pay?
